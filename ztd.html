What this shows

Straight overhead, the lower atmosphere adds about 2.4 m of apparent distance to a satellite signal. That’s the zenith tropospheric delay. Most of it comes from the dry gases in the air, and a smaller, highly variable part comes from water vapor — which is what makes the delay useful for weather forecasting.

A satellite lower in the sky sends its signal through more air, so the slant delay grows. My research recovers the zenith delay from many satellites at once and measures how accurately it can be estimated, in simulation, at both GPS frequencies (1.5 GHz) and Ku-band (12 GHz).
